\section{Executive summary}\label{executive-summary}

{[}Established{]} In classical fixed-design Gaussian regression with
$p<n$, exact finite-sample inference is routine for the coefficient
vector and for linear restrictions: Student $t$, Fisher $F$, the
joint $F$-ellipsoid, and Scheffé simultaneous contrast regions are
exact conditional on the design. These objects concern coefficients or
linear hypotheses. By themselves, they do not provide an informative
upper confidence set for the exact support.

{[}Established{]} Most Lasso inference results fall into one of four
classes: finite-sample estimation or prediction bounds; asymptotic
support recovery; asymptotic debiased-Lasso coefficient inference; or
exact conditional inference for selected-model coefficients after a
fixed selection event. None of these classes, without an additional
argument, proves the target

$$
\Pr\!\left(S^\star\subseteq U_n\right)\ge 1-\alpha
$$

for a useful support upper set at a user-chosen finite $n$. In
particular, a confidence interval that excludes zero can certify
activity, while certifying inactivity requires ruling out every nonzero
value. These are opposite inferential directions.

{[}Established{]} Exact $\ell_0$-constrained optimization is now
computationally realistic in many instances, but the core optimization
papers do not supply inferential coverage. Selection-consistency results
for best-subset or constrained regression are generally asymptotic and
require separation, beta-min, or related identifiability assumptions.
Exact selective intervals after best-subset selection target
coefficients conditional on the selection event, not the unknown support
itself.

{[}Established{]} The closest established fixed-sample result found is
Wang, Xie, and Zhang (2026), \emph{Finite- and Large-Sample Inference
for Model and Coefficients in High-dimensional Linear Regression with
Repro Samples}. Their Theorem 7 gives an exact confidence collection for
the true sparse model under its stated pivotal-conditioning assumptions.
Their practical candidate-restricted construction has the weaker
guarantee in Theorem 8: for fixed finite $n,p$, coverage approaches
its nominal level as the number $d$ of repro samples grows, with an
explicit candidate-omission component and an asymptotic computational
remainder. Theorem 9 is asymptotic in $n$ for fixed $d$.

{[}Deduction{]} Any confidence collection $\Gamma_n$ of supports
satisfying $\Pr(S^\star\in\Gamma_n)\ge1-\alpha$ immediately gives a
support upper set

$$
U_n=\bigcup_{S\in\Gamma_n}S,
\qquad
\Pr(S^\star\subseteq U_n)\ge1-\alpha.
$$

Thus Wang, Xie, and Zhang's exact Theorem 7 solves the requested
coverage problem in principle after this union map. Its practical
usefulness depends on whether the resulting union is smaller than
$[p]$ and whether the exact full-space set can be computed.

{[}Established{]} Anytime-valid ordinary linear regression is
substantially more developed than anytime-valid support inference.
Self-normalized confidence ellipsoids, likelihood-ratio martingales,
invariant sequential $t$- and $F$-type procedures, and general
sequential likelihood-mixing confidence sequences provide time-uniform
confidence regions for coefficients or groups of coefficients. The main
sources audited were Abbasi-Yadkori, Pál, and Szepesvári (2011), Lindon,
Ham, Tingley, and Bojinov (2026), and Kirschner, Krause, Meziu, and
Mutný (2025).

{[}Deduction{]} A coefficient confidence sequence $C_t$ gives a
formally valid support upper sequence through

$$
U_t=\bigcup_{\beta\in C_t}\operatorname{supp}(\beta).
$$

The missing step in the existing regression literature is therefore not
coverage logic. The missing step is informativeness and computation: a
full-dimensional confidence ellipsoid contains vectors with every
coordinate nonzero, so its support union is exactly $[p]$.

{[}Original result --- proof complete{]} This report proves a boundary
impossibility result. Over a continuous Gaussian regression parameter
space that permits a zero coefficient to be perturbed to an arbitrarily
small nonzero coefficient, any uniformly valid procedure for certifying
that coordinate as exactly zero has certification probability at most
$\alpha$ at the zero boundary. Therefore nontrivial exact-zero
certification requires a separation restriction, such as a beta-min gap;
a discrete coefficient model; exact known sparsity; a practically-zero
equivalence region; or a weaker, nonuniform notion of validity.

{[}Original result --- proof complete{]} Under fixed design, Gaussian
noise, and known variance, an exact finite-sample construction is
obtained from the residual ball

$$
C_n=\left\{\beta\in\Theta:
  \lVert Y-X\beta\rVert_2^2
  \le \sigma^2\chi^2_{n,1-\alpha}
\right\},
\qquad
U_n=\widehat S_n\cup\bigcup_{\beta\in C_n}\operatorname{supp}(\beta).
$$

It has conditional-on-$X$ coverage at least $1-\alpha$ for any
selector $\widehat S_n$, including Lasso with cross-validated tuning
or an exact $\ell_0$ solution. It becomes computationally and
statistically meaningful when $\Theta$ imposes explicit sparsity and
separation, for example $\lVert\beta\rVert_0\le k$,
$\lVert\beta\rVert_2\le B$, and each nonzero coefficient has magnitude
at least $b>0$.

{[}Original result --- proof complete{]} An anytime-valid construction
follows from a prequential likelihood-ratio martingale. Any predictable
sequence of regression predictors---including online Lasso or online
exact best subset---can be used in the numerator. Inverting the
resulting process gives a joint parameter confidence sequence, which can
then be mapped to a support upper sequence. Validity does not require
the predictor to select the true model; prediction quality controls
tightness, while martingale normalization controls coverage.

{[}Established{]} The main scientific conclusion is therefore
conditional. Exact fixed-sample and anytime-valid support upper sets
exist as mathematical constructions. Without an identifiability or
separation restriction they are generally vacuous or nearly powerless.
The central unresolved problem is to obtain a computationally practical,
nonvacuous certificate under assumptions that are both scientifically
credible and checkable enough for the intended application.

\section{1. Statistical target and
notation}\label{statistical-target-and-notation}

{[}Established{]} Let $X_n\in\mathbb R^{n\times p}$,
$Y_n\in\mathbb R^n$, and

$$
Y_n=X_n\beta^\star+\varepsilon_n,
\qquad
S^\star=\{j:\beta_j^\star\ne0\}.
$$

{[}Established{]} A sparse algorithm returns a raw selected set
$\widehat S_n$. A confidence certificate is a different object: an
upper confidence set $U_n\subseteq[p]$ for $S^\star$, with certified
inactive set $Z_n=U_n^c$. The required fixed-sample guarantee is

$$
\Pr(S^\star\subseteq U_n)\ge1-\alpha,
$$

or equivalently

$$
\Pr\{Z_n\subseteq(S^\star)^c\}\ge1-\alpha.
$$

{[}Established{]} The anytime-valid target is

$$
\Pr\left(\forall t\ge1:S^\star\subseteq U_t\right)\ge1-\alpha.
$$

{[}Established{]} This event implies validity at every stopping time
$\tau$. It does not imply that a retained feature is active. A lower
support set, which certifies activity, is a different inferential
object.

{[}Established{]} The Lasso and exact constrained estimators are

$$
\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{Lasso},n}
\in\arg\min_{\beta\in\mathbb R^p}
\left\{\frac12\lVert Y_n-X_n\beta\rVert_2^2+\lambda\lVert\beta\rVert_1\right\}
$$

and

$$
\widehat\beta_{0,n}
\in\arg\min_{\beta\in\mathbb R^p}
\left\{\frac12\lVert Y_n-X_n\beta\rVert_2^2:
\lVert\beta\rVert_0\le k\right\}.
$$

{[}Established{]} Deterministic tie-breaking is assumed whenever an
estimator is referred to as a function of the data. An approximate
best-subset algorithm is not called an exact global $\ell_0$ solution.

\section{3. Stage 1 --- established fixed-sample and asymptotic
results}\label{stage-1-established-fixed-sample-and-asymptotic-results}

\subsection{3.1 Classical fixed-design Gaussian linear
regression}\label{classical-fixed-design-gaussian-linear-regression}

{[}Established{]} Assume $X$ is fixed, $\operatorname{rank}(X)=p<n$,
and $\varepsilon\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_n)$. Let

$$
\widehat\beta=(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top Y,
\qquad
s^2=\frac{\lVert Y-X\widehat\beta\rVert_2^2}{n-p}.
$$

Then

$$
\widehat\beta\sim N\!\left(\beta^\star,\sigma^2(X^\top X)^{-1}\right),
\qquad
\frac{(n-p)s^2}{\sigma^2}\sim\chi^2_{n-p},
$$

and the two quantities are independent.

{[}Established{]} For coordinate $j$, with
$v_{jj}=((X^\top X)^{-1})_{jj}$,

$$
\frac{\widehat\beta_j-\beta_j^\star}{s\sqrt{v_{jj}}}\sim t_{n-p}.
$$

This gives an exact coordinate interval conditional on $X$. Bonferroni
or a joint multivariate calculation gives simultaneous coordinate
coverage.

{[}Established{]} For a rank-$q$ matrix $R$, the statistic

$$
\frac{(R\widehat\beta-r)^\top
[R(X^\top X)^{-1}R^\top]^{-1}
(R\widehat\beta-r)}{q s^2}
$$

has an exact $F_{q,n-p}$ distribution under $R\beta^\star=r$.
Inverting this test gives exact confidence regions for linear
restrictions.

{[}Established{]} The joint coefficient ellipsoid is

$$
\mathcal E_{1-\alpha}=
\left\{\beta:
(\widehat\beta-\beta)^\top X^\top X(\widehat\beta-\beta)
\le p s^2F_{p,n-p;1-\alpha}
\right\}.
$$

Scheffé's method gives simultaneous protection for every contrast
$c^\top\beta$.

{[}Deduction{]} These exact regions do not ordinarily yield useful zero
certificates. A nondegenerate ellipsoid has nonempty interior; hence it
contains a vector with coordinate $j$ nonzero for every $j$. Its
support union is $[p]$.

{[}Established{]} Under independent but non-Gaussian or heteroskedastic
errors, ordinary least squares with sandwich covariance estimation
supports asymptotic coefficient inference under moment, leverage, and
central-limit conditions. Such intervals are not exact finite-sample
Gaussian pivots and are not automatically valid after arbitrary
data-dependent model selection.

{[}Established{]} Berk et al.~(2013) construct post-selection-valid
intervals by simultaneous protection over coefficients arising from all
candidate submodels. The procedure provides finite-sample Gaussian
post-selection protection in its exact setup, but its target is a
model-dependent projection coefficient, not the exact support of a
single true sparse vector.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{3.2 Lasso and related $\ell_1$
methods}{3.2 Lasso and related \textbackslash ell\_1 methods}}\label{lasso-and-related-ell_1-methods}

{[}Established{]} Finite-sample Lasso oracle inequalities control
prediction error, $\ell_1$ error, or $\ell_2$ error under
deterministic tuning and design conditions such as restricted eigenvalue
or compatibility assumptions. These are estimation statements, not
confidence sets for $S^\star$.

{[}Established{]} Zhao and Yu (2006) establish that an irrepresentable
condition is almost necessary and sufficient for asymptotic
model-selection consistency of the Lasso in their regimes. Wainwright
(2009) gives sharp sparsity-recovery thresholds and high-probability
analyses under Gaussian designs and beta-min conditions. These results
are about recovery probability under a model sequence; they do not,
without calibration and known constants, constitute a generally
available finite-sample confidence certificate at arbitrary $\alpha$.

{[}Established{]} Sure screening means
$\Pr(S^\star\subseteq\widehat S_n)\to1$. Fan and Lv (2008) give a
canonical sure-independence-screening result. Sure screening is in the
same direction as the requested upper-set guarantee, but its asymptotic
limit is not an exact finite-$n$ coverage statement.

{[}Established{]} Debiased or desparsified Lasso methods, including
Zhang and Zhang (2014) and van de Geer et al.~(2014), construct
asymptotically normal estimators for low-dimensional coordinates or
contrasts under sparsity and precision-matrix assumptions. Simultaneous
variants require high-dimensional Gaussian approximation or multiplier
bootstrap conditions. These procedures primarily certify activity when
zero is excluded; they do not certify exact inactivity.

{[}Established{]} Lee et al.~(2016) derive exact conditional laws after
a fixed-$\lambda$ Lasso selection event under Gaussian errors. The
inferential target is a selected-model projection coefficient after
conditioning on the polyhedral selection event. A variable not selected
by the Lasso is not thereby certified inactive in the full
data-generating coefficient vector.

{[}Established{]} A theorem proved for deterministic $\lambda$ cannot
be transferred to cross-validated $\lambda$ without including the
tuning rule in the selection event, using independent tuning data, or
providing a separate proof. Cross-validation changes the joint
data-dependent selection map.

{[}Established{]} Nickl and van de Geer (2013) construct and delimit
honest adaptive confidence sets for sparse coefficient vectors. Their
results identify separation/testing regimes needed for adaptation.
Pötscher (2009) and Pötscher and Schneider (2010) show that uniformly
valid confidence sets centered on sparse or penalized estimators can be
necessarily large. These results explain why pointwise oracle behavior
does not imply uniformly informative uncertainty quantification.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{3.3 Exact $\ell_0$-constrained regression
and best-subset
selection}{3.3 Exact \textbackslash ell\_0-constrained regression and best-subset selection}}\label{exact-ell_0-constrained-regression-and-best-subset-selection}

{[}Established{]} Bertsimas, King, and Mazumder (2016), and Bertsimas
and Van Parys (2020), establish modern exact optimization formulations
and scalable algorithms for best-subset regression. Their guarantees
concern optimization, optimality gaps, and statistical or computational
behavior; they are not confidence-coverage theorems for the unknown
support.

{[}Established{]} Shen, Pan, Zhu, and Zhou (2013) analyze constrained
and regularized high-dimensional regression and give
selection-consistency results under separation and growth conditions.
The relevant support conclusions are asymptotic.

{[}Established{]} Lin and Li (2023 preprint) derive finite-sample
selective confidence intervals after AIC-based best-subset selection by
characterizing the selection event. The intervals concern coefficients
conditional on selection, not a confidence collection for the true
support.

{[}Established{]} If $k<|S^\star|$, the exact $\ell_0$-constrained
estimator is fitted under a misspecified parameter space. No procedure
whose candidate supports are all constrained to size at most $k$ can
contain the original $S^\star$. If $k=|S^\star|$, exact-size
information removes one boundary ambiguity. If only $k\ge|S^\star|$ is
known, valid model collections are possible but their unions can be
large because supermodels of $S^\star$ remain admissible.

\subsection{3.4 Confidence collections of models and
supports}\label{confidence-collections-of-models-and-supports}

{[}Established{]} Ferrari and Yang (2015) construct a variable-selection
confidence set by retaining submodels not rejected against a classical
full model by $F$-testing. Under their fixed-design Gaussian
assumptions, the true model is contained at the nominal level. The
method is low-dimensional because it requires a valid encompassing full
model.

{[}Deduction{]} The direct support union of Ferrari and Yang's full
variable-selection confidence set is vacuous: the full model is the
reference and is retained, so the union is $[p]$. Their lower-boundary
models are useful summaries of parsimony but do not by themselves
inherit the one-sided upper-support guarantee.

{[}Established{]} Li et al.~(2019) introduce lower and upper model
confidence bounds $m_L\subseteq m^\star\subseteq m_U$. Their bootstrap
theory is asymptotic and relies on selector and bootstrap conditions.
Leeb, Pötscher, and Kivaranovic (2019) identify nonuniformity and
selector-dependent difficulties in the associated discussion.

{[}Established{]} Battey (2023) studies high-dimensional coefficient
inference and confidence sets of low-dimensional models after
preliminary reduction. The model-set calibration used in that line is
asymptotic and depends on the reduction and encompassing-model
construction; it is not an exact finite-sample support upper set over
unrestricted $p\gg n$.

{[}Established{]} Wang, Xie, and Zhang (2026) define the target true
model as the unique smallest support generating the mean, addressing
high-dimensional nonidentifiability by assumption. Their Theorem 7
constructs a generic level-$c$ model confidence set
$\Gamma_c^\tau(Y)$ satisfying

$$
\Pr\{S^\star\in\Gamma_c^\tau(Y)\}\ge c,
$$

provided the stated conditional statistic is nuisance-free and its
acceptance set has conditional probability at least $c$.

{[}Established{]} Their candidate-generation Theorem 1 gives, for
Gaussian errors and its tuning interval,

$$
\Pr\{S^\star\notin\mathcal S^{(d)}\}
\le
\left(1-\frac{\gamma_\delta^{n-1}}{n-1}\right)^d+\delta,
$$

for finite $n,p$, with $n-|S^\star|>4$. The tuning interval depends
on $\sigma_0^2$, the true support size, and a separation quantity
$C_{\min}$.

{[}Established{]} Their Theorem 8 states, in the paper's convention
where $c$ is the coverage level, that the practical
candidate-restricted set has coverage at least

$$
c-\delta-o(e^{-c_1d})
$$

for fixed finite $n,p$ as $d\to\infty$. Their Theorem 9 gives
$c-o(e^{-c_2n})$ as $n\to\infty$ for fixed $d$, under the
corresponding large-sample conditions.

{[}Deduction{]} Theorem 7 is an exact fixed-sample confidence-collection
result in principle. Theorem 8 is finite-sample in the statistical
dimension but asymptotic in computational effort $d$. A finite
implemented value of $d$, plus finite Monte Carlo evaluation of
conditional tail probabilities, needs an explicit error budget before
exact nominal coverage can be claimed.

\subsection{3.6 Stage 1 completion gate}\label{stage-1-completion-gate}

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Coefficient inference only.} Classical $t/F$
regions, PoSI, debiased Lasso, selective Lasso, and selective
best-subset intervals provide coefficient or projection-parameter
inference. They do not directly certify that excluded variables have
coefficient exactly zero.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Support recovery only in the limit.} Zhao and
Yu, Wainwright's asymptotic threshold theory, Fan and Lv's sure
screening, and Shen et al.'s constrained-regression theory provide
limiting support statements under signal and design restrictions.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Honest finite-sample coverage.} Classical
Gaussian regions, exact selective Gaussian intervals for their
conditional targets, Ferrari and Yang's classical model collection,
universal-inference confidence sets, and Wang, Xie, and Zhang's generic
Theorem 7 are finite-sample objects under their stated assumptions. Of
these, Wang et al.~is the closest high-dimensional model-support result.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Confidence collection rather than one upper
set.} Ferrari and Yang, Battey's model-set line, and Wang et al.~return
collections of candidate models. A union map is required to obtain one
support upper set.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Closest result to the target.} Wang, Xie, and
Zhang (2026), Theorem 7, is the closest exact high-dimensional result.
Their Theorem 8 is the closest computational implementation, but its
displayed guarantee takes $d\to\infty$ for fixed $n,p$.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Constraining impossibility and identifiability
results.} Nonidentifiability of supports when columns span the same mean
vectors, honest-adaptation lower bounds, and the large-confidence-set
results of Pötscher and collaborators constrain any useful uniformly
valid procedure. The boundary theorem proved in Section 7 strengthens
the interpretation for exact-zero certification.

\section{4. Stage 2 --- anytime-valid inference for linear
regression}\label{stage-2-anytime-valid-inference-for-linear-regression}

\subsection{4.1 Core definitions}\label{core-definitions}

{[}Established{]} For a null family $\mathcal H$, an
\textbf{e-variable} is a nonnegative random variable $E$ satisfying

$$
\sup_{P\in\mathcal H}\mathbb E_P[E]\le1.
$$

A realized value much larger than one is evidence against
$\mathcal H$.

{[}Established{]} An \textbf{e-process} is an adapted nonnegative
process $(E_t)$ that retains the e-value property under arbitrary
stopping, in the operational form

$$
\sup_{P\in\mathcal H}\mathbb E_P[E_\tau]\le1
$$

for every stopping time $\tau$ for which the expression is defined. A
nonnegative test supermartingale starting at one is a canonical
e-process.

{[}Established{]} A \textbf{test martingale} is a nonnegative martingale
$(M_t)$ with $M_0=1$ under the null. A \textbf{test supermartingale}
replaces equality of conditional expectations by the supermartingale
inequality. For a composite null, validity must hold for every
distribution in the null family.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Ville's inequality} states that a nonnegative
supermartingale with initial value at most one satisfies

$$
P\!\left(\sup_{t\ge0}M_t\ge\frac1\alpha\right)\le\alpha.
$$

{[}Established{]} An always-valid $p$-value process can be obtained
from a test process by

$$
p_t=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{\sup_{s\le t}M_s}\right\}.
$$

{[}Established{]} A $(1-\alpha)$ \textbf{confidence sequence} is a
sequence $(C_t)$ satisfying

$$
P(\forall t\ge1:\theta^\star\in C_t)\ge1-\alpha.
$$

It remains valid at every stopping time and under optional continuation
because its guarantee is a single time-uniform event.

{[}Established{]} A fixed-time e-value is not automatically an
e-process. Reusing, peeking at, or multiplying fixed-time e-values
across times requires a valid sequential construction.

{[}Established{]} For a simple null $P_0$ and alternative density
$Q$, the likelihood ratio $dQ/dP_0$ is an e-variable; its sequential
product is a test martingale when the factors are predictable normalized
densities. Mixture likelihood ratios integrate alternatives. Universal
inference uses held-out or prequential likelihood ratios so that an
estimated alternative appears in the numerator without invalidating
normalization. Self-normalized methods build exponential
supermartingales that avoid direct knowledge of a variance scale in
selected settings.

\subsection{4.2 Established anytime-valid regression
results}\label{established-anytime-valid-regression-results}

{[}Established{]} Abbasi-Yadkori, Pál, and Szepesvári (2011) prove a
time-uniform self-normalized confidence ellipsoid for regularized least
squares with predictable covariates and conditionally sub-Gaussian
noise. Their Theorem 2 is stated with a known sub-Gaussian scale and a
known bound on $\lVert\theta^\star\rVert$. The object is a coefficient
ellipsoid.

{[}Established{]} Lindon, Ham, Tingley, and Bojinov (2026) construct
exact parametric sequential analogues of classical linear-model tests
using likelihood ratios of invariantly sufficient statistics. Their
parametric results include unknown variance and nuisance regression
coefficients, sequential $F$-type tests, and coefficient or group
confidence sequences. The paper then gives heteroskedasticity-robust
model-free causal procedures that are asymptotically anytime-valid
rather than exact parametric finite-sample procedures.

{[}Established{]} Kirschner, Krause, Meziu, and Mutný (2025 preprint)
give a general realizable-likelihood framework. Their Theorem 2 states
that a sequential likelihood-ratio sublevel set is a confidence sequence
for any adapted estimator sequence. Their Theorem 4 allows adapted
likelihood-mixing distributions. The framework permits arbitrary
dependence of the covariate on past observations, provided the
conditional likelihood is correctly specified.

{[}Established{]} In Kirschner et al.'s displayed Gaussian sequential
linear-regression specialization, the variance is known and the
confidence set is a regularized-least-squares parameter set. Their
sparse section restricts the parameter space to a bounded $k$-sparse
set and converts online regret to a confidence sequence. The paper
explicitly notes computational difficulty of sparse maximum likelihood
or an equally strong sparse online predictor.

{[}Established{]} de la Peña, Klass, and Lai (2004) provide
self-normalized process inequalities that underlie many sequential
regression bounds. Howard et al.~(2020, 2021) provide general
nonnegative-supermartingale and confidence-sequence construction tools.
Ramdas et al.~(2023) organize these ideas as safe anytime-valid
inference.

{[}Established{]} Wang and Ramdas (2025) give anytime-valid $t$-tests
and confidence sequences for Gaussian means with unknown variance. This
is an important nuisance-variance building block, but it is not by
itself a general high-dimensional regression support certificate.

{[}Established{]} Universal inference gives finite-sample
split-likelihood e-values and confidence sets under weak regularity. A
single split likelihood ratio is a fixed-sample e-value. It becomes
anytime-valid only when embedded in a valid sequential or
optional-continuation construction; the label ``e-process'' cannot be
inferred from the fixed-time expectation bound alone.

\subsection{4.4 Stage 2 completion gate}\label{stage-2-completion-gate}

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Genuinely time-uniform.} Abbasi-Yadkori et
al.'s ellipsoid, Lindon et al.'s exact parametric martingales and
inverted sets, Kirschner et al.'s sequential likelihood sets, and the
confidence-sequence results built from nonnegative supermartingales are
genuinely time-uniform. A conventional fixed-time $t$-test, a split
e-value, or an asymptotic interval at each time is not time-uniform
merely because it can be recomputed repeatedly.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Stopping-time validity.} Every result
expressed as one event $\{\forall t:\theta^\star\in C_t\}$ or
controlled by Ville's inequality remains valid at arbitrary stopping
times. Fixed-time asymptotic intervals generally do not.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Composite nulls and nuisance parameters.}
Lindon et al.~explicitly handle regression nuisance coefficients and
unknown variance through invariance. General likelihood mixing can
include nuisance parameters jointly, but a pointwise likelihood ratio is
not automatically a composite-null e-process. Universal inference
handles composite nulls by profiling or taking a null supremum, often
with a computational cost.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Coefficient regions but not support
certificates.} The audited regression papers return confidence regions
for $\beta$, a contrast, or a group. None advertises a directly
computed, nonvacuous set $U_t$ with simultaneous excluded-feature
certification over an unrestricted continuous sparse-regression
parameter space.

{[}Deduction{]} \textbf{Exact missing mathematical step.} Given a joint
confidence sequence $C_t$, the coverage step is only

$$
C_t\ni\beta^\star
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
S^\star\subseteq\bigcup_{\beta\in C_t}\operatorname{supp}(\beta).
$$

The unresolved steps are to restrict the parameter space so that the
union can omit coordinates, establish identifiability and separation,
and compute the coordinate-wise feasibility queries without enumerating
all supports.

\section{5. Precise gap statement}\label{precise-gap-statement}

{[}Established{]} Existing coefficient inference is naturally suited to
a \textbf{lower} support certificate

$$
L_n=\{j:0\notin I_{j,n}\},
$$

because simultaneous coefficient intervals imply
$L_n\subseteq S^\star$ with high probability. The requested object is
an \textbf{upper} support certificate. To place $j$ outside $U_n$,
the procedure must rule out every admissible parameter vector with
$\beta_j\ne0$.

{[}Deduction{]} For an ordinary full-dimensional confidence set, that
universal exclusion is impossible because arbitrarily small nonzero
perturbations remain inside the set. This explains why converting a
debiased-Lasso ellipsoid or an OLS ellipsoid to an upper support set is
formally valid but scientifically empty.

{[}Established{]} Model-confidence collections avoid the continuous
perturbation problem by treating support as a discrete target. They
still face two obstacles. First, support must be identifiable from the
mean representation. Second, a collection that includes many supermodels
can have union $[p]$, even while its model-level coverage is exact.

{[}Established{]} The literature gap is therefore not ``no finite-sample
confidence set exists.'' Wang, Xie, and Zhang (2026) now supply an exact
model-confidence construction. The remaining gap is an exact,
computationally feasible, nonvacuous support upper set for finite
computational budgets under transparent assumptions, together with an
anytime-valid analogue.

\section{6. Stage 3 --- assumption ladder and construction

{[}Established{]} The constructions below state their assumption level
explicitly.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  {[}Established{]} \textbf{Level 1:} fixed design, Gaussian noise,
  known variance, deterministic or predictable tuning/constraint.
\item
  {[}Established{]} \textbf{Level 2:} fixed design, Gaussian noise,
  unknown variance.
\item
  {[}Established{]} \textbf{Level 3:} random design with a stated

\item
  {[}Established{]} \textbf{Level 4:} predictable or adaptive covariates

\end{enumerate}

{[}Established{]} Coverage is separated from informativeness. A

fraction of truly inactive variables certified in simulations, and
computational cost.

\section{7. General lemmas and impossibility
results}\label{general-lemmas-and-impossibility-results}

\subsection{7.1 Model-confidence collection to support upper
set}\label{model-confidence-collection-to-support-upper-set}

\textbf{Lemma 1 {[}Deduction --- proof complete{]}.} Let $\Gamma$ be a
random collection of subsets of $[p]$. If

$$
\Pr(S^\star\in\Gamma)\ge1-\alpha,
$$

then

$$
U(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{S\in\Gamma}S
$$

satisfies $\Pr(S^\star\subseteq U(\Gamma))\ge1-\alpha$.

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Deduction{]} On the event $S^\star\in\Gamma$, every
element of $S^\star$ belongs to the union of all elements of
$\Gamma$. Hence

$$
\{S^\star\in\Gamma\}\subseteq\{S^\star\subseteq U(\Gamma)\}.
$$

Taking probabilities proves the claim. $\square$

\textbf{Anytime extension {[}Deduction --- proof complete{]}.} If

$$
\Pr(\forall t:S^\star\in\Gamma_t)\ge1-\alpha,
$$

then $U_t=\bigcup_{S\in\Gamma_t}S$ satisfies

$$
\Pr(\forall t:S^\star\subseteq U_t)\ge1-\alpha.
$$

\subsection{7.2 Parameter confidence set to support upper
set}\label{parameter-confidence-set-to-support-upper-set}

\textbf{Lemma 2 {[}Deduction --- proof complete{]}.} Let
$C\subseteq\Theta\subseteq\mathbb R^p$ be a random confidence set
satisfying

$$
\Pr_{\beta^\star}(\beta^\star\in C)\ge1-\alpha
\quad\text{for every }\beta^\star\in\Theta.
$$

Define

$$
U(C)=\bigcup_{\beta\in C}\operatorname{supp}(\beta).
$$

Then $\Pr_{\beta^\star}(S^\star\subseteq U(C))\ge1-\alpha$ for every
$\beta^\star\in\Theta$.

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Deduction{]} On $\{\beta^\star\in C\}$, the vector
$\beta^\star$ is one member of the union defining $U(C)$; therefore
$\operatorname{supp}(\beta^\star)\subseteq U(C)$. $\square$

\subsection{7.3 Selector wrapper}\label{selector-wrapper}

\textbf{Lemma 3 {[}Original result --- proof complete{]}.} If $U_0$ is
any valid support upper set and $A(Y)\subseteq[p]$ is any
data-dependent set, then

$$
U=A(Y)\cup U_0
$$

has at least the same support coverage.

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Original result --- proof complete{]} The event
$S^\star\subseteq U_0$ implies $S^\star\subseteq A\cup U_0$.
$\square$

{[}Deduction{]} Taking $A=\widehat S_{\mathrm{Lasso}}$ or
$A=\widehat S_0$ ensures that no feature retained by the raw sparse
algorithm is declared inactive, without requiring the selector itself to
be valid. The inferential burden is carried by $U_0$.

\subsection{7.4 Interior-vacuity
proposition}\label{interior-vacuity-proposition}

\textbf{Proposition 1 {[}Original result --- proof complete{]}.} If
$C\subseteq\mathbb R^p$ has nonempty Euclidean interior, then

$$
\bigcup_{\beta\in C}\operatorname{supp}(\beta)=[p].
$$

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Original result --- proof complete{]} Choose an open
ball $B(\beta_0,r)\subseteq C$. For any coordinate $j$, the ball
contains $\beta_0+te_j$ for all sufficiently small $|t|$. Choose
such a $t$ with $\beta_{0j}+t\ne0$. Thus coordinate $j$ appears in
the support of a member of $C$. Since this holds for every $j$, the
union is $[p]$. $\square$

{[}Deduction{]} Every nondegenerate OLS or regularized least-squares
ellipsoid in $\mathbb R^p$ has a vacuous support union. A useful
support map requires a nonconvex restricted parameter space or a
confidence object constructed directly in model space.

\subsection{7.5 Boundary impossibility for exact-zero
certification}\label{boundary-impossibility-for-exact-zero-certification}

\textbf{Theorem 1 {[}Original result --- proof complete{]}.} Fix a
finite design $X$ and known $\sigma>0$. Let
$Y\sim N(X\beta,\sigma^2I_n)$. Suppose a procedure returns
$Z(Y)\subseteq[p]$ and satisfies the uniform familywise correctness
condition

$$
\Pr_\beta\{Z(Y)\subseteq S(\beta)^c\}\ge1-\alpha
\quad\text{for every }\beta\in\Theta.
$$

Fix $\beta^0\in\Theta$ with $\beta_j^0=0$. Assume $\Theta$
contains a sequence $\beta^{(m)}\to\beta^0$ with
$\beta_j^{(m)}\ne0$. Then

$$
\Pr_{\beta^0}\{j\in Z(Y)\}\le\alpha.
$$

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Original result --- proof complete{]} For each $m$,
coordinate $j$ is active under $\beta^{(m)}$. Hence

$$
\{j\in Z(Y)\}
\subseteq
\{Z(Y)\not\subseteq S(\beta^{(m)})^c\},
$$

so uniform validity gives

$$
\Pr_{\beta^{(m)}}\{j\in Z(Y)\}\le\alpha.
$$

Because $\beta^{(m)}\to\beta^0$, the Gaussian mean vectors
$X\beta^{(m)}\to X\beta^0$. Gaussian laws with common nonsingular
covariance converge in total variation. Therefore probabilities of the
measurable event $\{j\in Z(Y)\}$ converge, yielding

$$
\Pr_{\beta^0}\{j\in Z(Y)\}
=
\lim_{m\to\infty}
\Pr_{\beta^{(m)}}\{j\in Z(Y)\}
\le\alpha.
$$

$\square$

{[}Established{]} The theorem applies to $\Theta=\mathbb R^p$, to
ordinary $\ell_0$-upper-bound classes at points with unused sparsity
capacity, and to many continuous sparse neighborhoods. It does not apply
when the parameter space forbids arbitrarily small nonzero coefficients,
for example through beta-min, or when exact known support size makes the
relevant perturbation leave the parameter space.

{[}Deduction{]} At a genuine zero on the boundary of active
alternatives, a uniformly honest exact-zero certificate can succeed with
probability no greater than the allowed familywise error. This is
stronger than merely saying that intervals are wide: it identifies
exact-zero certification as a nonregular one-sided decision problem.

\subsection{7.6 Nonidentifiable columns}\label{nonidentifiable-columns}

\textbf{Proposition 2 {[}Original result --- proof complete{]}.} If two
parameter vectors $\beta^{(1)}$ and $\beta^{(2)}$ satisfy
$X\beta^{(1)}=X\beta^{(2)}$ but have different supports, then no
data-based procedure can distinguish the two support truths from the
distribution of $Y$.

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Original result --- proof complete{]} Under a common
noise law, the two response distributions are identical. Every event has
the same probability under both parameters. Any coordinate active in one
representation and inactive in the other must therefore be treated
conservatively if coverage is required for both. $\square$

{[}Deduction{]} Deterministic tie-breaking makes an optimizer
single-valued but does not make the true support identifiable. A target
such as the unique smallest support, as used by Wang, Xie, and Zhang, is
an additional statistical assumption.

\section{8. Fixed-sample
constructions}\label{fixed-sample-constructions}

\subsection{8.1 Exact residual-ball certificate: known
variance}\label{exact-residual-ball-certificate-known-variance}

\textbf{Theorem 2 {[}Original result --- proof complete; assumption
level 1{]}.} Let $X\in\mathbb R^{n\times p}$ be fixed, let

$$
Y=X\beta^\star+\varepsilon,
\qquad
\varepsilon\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_n),
$$

with known $\sigma$, and assume
$\beta^\star\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb R^p$. Let $q_{n,1-\alpha}$ be
the $(1-\alpha)$-quantile of $\chi_n^2$. Define

$$
C_{\alpha}(Y;\Theta)
=
\left\{\beta\in\Theta:
\lVert Y-X\beta\rVert_2^2
\le\sigma^2q_{n,1-\alpha}
\right\},
$$

$$
U_{0,\alpha}(Y)
=
\bigcup_{\beta\in C_\alpha(Y;\Theta)}
\operatorname{supp}(\beta),
$$

and, for any raw selector $\widehat S(Y)$,

$$
U_\alpha(Y)=\widehat S(Y)\cup U_{0,\alpha}(Y).
$$

Then, conditional on $X$,

$$
\Pr_{\beta^\star}\{S^\star\subseteq U_\alpha(Y)\}\ge1-\alpha.
$$

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Original result --- proof complete{]} At the true
coefficient,

$$
\lVert Y-X\beta^\star\rVert_2^2
=
\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_2^2,
\qquad
\frac{\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_2^2}{\sigma^2}\sim\chi_n^2.
$$

Thus $\Pr(\beta^\star\in C_\alpha)=1-\alpha$. Lemma 2 gives support
coverage for $U_{0,\alpha}$, and Lemma 3 preserves it after adjoining
$\widehat S$. $\square$

{[}Established{]} The theorem does not require $p<n$, full column
rank, restricted eigenvalues, correct Lasso selection, or exact
$\ell_0$ recovery. It is uniform over $\Theta$ under the stated
Gaussian model.

{[}Deduction{]} If $\Theta=\mathbb R^p$, the certificate is normally
vacuous by Proposition 1 or by nonidentifiability. The useful version
restricts $\Theta$.

\subsection{8.2 Closed sparse beta-min parameter space and coordinate
feasibility}\label{closed-sparse-beta-min-parameter-space-and-coordinate-feasibility}

{[}Original result --- proof complete{]} Define

$$
\Theta_{k,b,B}
=
\left\{\beta\in\mathbb R^p:
\lVert\beta\rVert_0\le k,
\ \lVert\beta\rVert_2\le B,
\ \beta_j=0\text{ or }|\beta_j|\ge b\ \forall j
\right\},
$$

where $1\le k<n$, $b>0$, and $B<\infty$ are prespecified and are
assumed to contain $\beta^\star$.

{[}Original result --- proof complete{]} For coordinate $j$, let

$$
r_j(Y)=
\min_{\beta\in\Theta_{k,b,B}:\ |\beta_j|\ge b}
\lVert Y-X\beta\rVert_2^2,
$$

with $r_j=+\infty$ if the feasible set is empty. Compactness ensures
the minimum exists. Then

$$
j\in U_{0,\alpha}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r_j(Y)\le\sigma^2q_{n,1-\alpha}.
$$

{[}Deduction{]} A Lasso-excluded feature
$j\notin\widehat S_{\mathrm{Lasso}}$ is certified inactive exactly
when $r_j>\sigma^2q_{n,1-\alpha}$. No multiplicity correction is
needed because all coordinates are derived from one joint confidence
event.

{[}Established{]} Each $r_j$ can be formulated as two mixed-integer
quadratic programs, one imposing $\beta_j\ge b$ and one imposing
$\beta_j\le-b$, together with sparsity and norm constraints. This
avoids explicit enumeration of $2^p$ supports but remains NP-hard in
the worst case. The bound $B$ supplies finite big-$M$ constants when
a big-$M$ formulation is used.

{[}Deduction{]} The beta-min gap is not a cosmetic power condition. It
closes the active-coordinate alternative set and removes the local
perturbations in Theorem 1. Without it, the feasibility infimum for
$\beta_j\ne0$ can equal the value at $\beta_j=0$, making exact
exclusion unstable.

\subsection{8.3 Unknown variance with an independent scale
estimate}\label{unknown-variance-with-an-independent-scale-estimate}

\textbf{Theorem 3 {[}Original result --- proof complete; assumption
level 2 with external scale information{]}.} In the setup of Theorem 2,
suppose $\sigma$ is unknown but an independent estimator
$\widetilde\sigma^2$ satisfies

$$
\frac{\nu\widetilde\sigma^2}{\sigma^2}\sim\chi_\nu^2.
$$

Define

$$
C_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{ind}\text{-}F}
=
\left\{\beta\in\Theta:
\lVert Y-X\beta\rVert_2^2
\le
n\widetilde\sigma^2F_{n,\nu;1-\alpha}
\right\}.
$$

Then
$\Pr(\beta^\star\in C_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{ind}\text{-}F})=1-\alpha$, and
its support union, with or without a selector wrapper, is an exact
support upper set.

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Original result --- proof complete{]} Independence
gives

$$
\frac{\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_2^2/n}{\widetilde\sigma^2}
=
\frac{(\chi_n^2/n)}{(\chi_\nu^2/\nu)}
\sim F_{n,\nu}.
$$

The remaining argument is Lemmas 2 and 3. $\square$

{[}Established{]} In ordinary $p<n$ regression, the classical joint
$F$-ellipsoid supplies an exact unknown-variance coefficient set using
the same sample. Intersecting it with a separated sparse parameter space
gives a valid support upper set. In $p\ge n$, a full-model residual
variance estimate generally does not exist without additional structure;
Wang et al.'s conditioning approach or a joint likelihood construction
is then more relevant.

\subsection{8.4 Exact residual model collection when the support-size
class is
specified}\label{exact-residual-model-collection-when-the-support-size-class-is-specified}

\textbf{Theorem 4 {[}Original result --- proof complete; assumption
level 1{]}.} Let $\mathcal M$ be a prespecified collection of supports
$S\subseteq[p]$ such that $\operatorname{rank}(X_S)=|S|<n$, and
assume $S^\star\in\mathcal M$. Let

$$
P_S=X_S(X_S^\top X_S)^{-1}X_S^\top,
\qquad
\operatorname{RSS}_S=\lVert(I-P_S)Y\rVert_2^2.
$$

Define

$$
\Gamma_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{RSS}}
=
\left\{S\in\mathcal M:
\operatorname{RSS}_S
\le
\sigma^2\chi^2_{n-|S|,1-\alpha}
\right\}.
$$

Then

$$
\Pr(S^\star\in\Gamma_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{RSS}})=1-\alpha,
$$

and $U=\bigcup_{S\in\Gamma_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{RSS}}}S$ is an exact
support upper set.

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Original result --- proof complete{]} Since
$X\beta^\star\in\operatorname{col}(X_{S^\star})$,

$$
(I-P_{S^\star})Y=(I-P_{S^\star})\varepsilon.
$$

The projection $I-P_{S^\star}$ has rank $n-|S^\star|$, so

$$
\operatorname{RSS}_{S^\star}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-|S^\star|}.
$$

This gives exact model inclusion. Lemma 1 gives support coverage.
$\square$

{[}Deduction{]} No multiple-testing correction is required for coverage
of the one true support: only the acceptance probability of $S^\star$
matters. Multiplicity would matter for other simultaneous
interpretations of the accepted models.

{[}Established{]} If $\mathcal M=\{S:|S|=k\}$ and $k=|S^\star|$,
membership of a coordinate in $U$ can be checked by solving an exact
best-subset residual problem constrained to include that coordinate. If
$\mathcal M=\{S:|S|\le k\}$ with $k>|S^\star|$, the result remains
valid but can be uninformative because many supermodels are accepted. If
$k<|S^\star|$, the assumption $S^\star\in\mathcal M$ fails and so
does the coverage conclusion.

{[}Deduction{]} The same theorem with an independent variance estimate
replaces the chi-square cutoff by

$$
(n-|S|)\widetilde\sigma^2F_{n-|S|,\nu;1-\alpha}.
$$

\subsection{8.5 Error-budgeted use of a candidate support
set}\label{error-budgeted-use-of-a-candidate-support-set}

\textbf{Lemma 4 {[}Original result --- proof complete{]}.} Suppose a
random candidate set $\mathcal S$ satisfies

$$
\Pr(S^\star\notin\mathcal S)\le\eta,
$$

and a full-space confidence collection $\Gamma$ satisfies

$$
\Pr(S^\star\notin\Gamma)\le\xi.
$$

Then $\Gamma\cap\mathcal S$ contains $S^\star$ with probability at
least $1-\eta-\xi$.

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Original result --- proof complete{]} The failure
event is contained in

$$
\{S^\star\notin\mathcal S\}\cup\{S^\star\notin\Gamma\},
$$

and the union bound applies. $\square$

{[}Deduction{]} A finite-computation repro-sample implementation could
be made exactly $(1-\alpha)$-valid if it supplied a computable finite
bound $\eta$, an exact conditional confidence collection with error
$\xi$, and any Monte Carlo error $\zeta$, with
$\eta+\xi+\zeta\le\alpha$.

{[}Established{]} Wang et al.'s Theorem 1 supplies a finite
candidate-omission expression, but its admissible tuning interval
depends on unknown $\sigma_0^2$, $|S^\star|$, and $C_{\min}$.
Therefore it is not directly an implementable user-level error budget
without additional known bounds or conservative estimation.

{[}Conjecture{]} Exact conditional Monte Carlo ranks, with an add-one
correction and explicit randomization, may remove an unaccounted finite
simulation error in the Fisher-Dempster inversion step. A proof would
need conditional exchangeability of the observed and simulated
statistics after every data-dependent candidate and nuisance operation.
That proof is not supplied here.

\section{9. Anytime-valid construction by prequential likelihood
inversion}\label{anytime-valid-construction-by-prequential-likelihood-inversion}

\subsection{9.1 Generic theorem}\label{generic-theorem}

\textbf{Theorem 5 {[}Original synthesis --- proof complete; assumption
level 4{]}.} Let $\mathcal F_{t-1}$ contain all observations through
time $t-1$, and let $x_t$ be $\mathcal F_{t-1}$-measurable or
revealed before $Y_t$. Let

$$
Y_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},x_t
\sim p_{\theta^\star}(\cdot\mid x_t),
\qquad
\theta^\star=(\beta^\star,\eta^\star)\in\Theta,
$$

where $\eta$ denotes any nuisance parameter included in the
conditional likelihood. Let $q_{t-1}(\cdot\mid x_t)$ be any normalized
$\mathcal F_{t-1}$-measurable predictive density with respect to the
same dominating measure. Ratios are evaluated on the support of
$p_\theta$; a candidate $\theta$ assigning zero density to an
observed response is rejected. Define, for each $\theta\in\Theta$,

$$
E_t(\theta)
=
\prod_{s=1}^t
\frac{q_{s-1}(Y_s\mid x_s)}
     {p_\theta(Y_s\mid x_s)},
\qquad
C_t=\left\{\theta\in\Theta:E_t(\theta)<\frac1\alpha\right\}.
$$

Then

$$
\Pr_{\theta^\star}(\forall t\ge1:\theta^\star\in C_t)
\ge1-\alpha.
$$

Let $A_t\subseteq[p]$ be any adapted raw selected set and define

$$
U_t=A_t\cup
\bigcup_{(\beta,\eta)\in C_t}
\operatorname{supp}(\beta).
$$

Then

$$
\Pr_{\theta^\star}(\forall t\ge1:S^\star\subseteq U_t)
\ge1-\alpha.
$$

\textbf{Proof.} {[}Original synthesis --- proof complete{]} At the
truth, the one-step factor has conditional expectation

$$
\mathbb E_{\theta^\star}
\left[
\left.
\frac{q_{t-1}(Y_t\mid x_t)}
     {p_{\theta^\star}(Y_t\mid x_t)}
\right|\mathcal F_{t-1},x_t
\right]
=
\int_{\{p_{\theta^\star}>0\}}
q_{t-1}(y\mid x_t)\,dy
\le 1.
$$

Equality holds when the predictive density is supported wherever the
true model density is positive, as in the Gaussian applications below.
Therefore $E_t(\theta^\star)$ is a nonnegative supermartingale
starting at one; under common support it is a martingale. Ville's
inequality gives

$$
\Pr\left(\exists t:E_t(\theta^\star)\ge1/\alpha\right)\le\alpha,
$$

which proves the confidence-sequence statement. On its complement,
$\theta^\star\in C_t$ for every $t$; Lemma 2 and the selector
wrapper then give the support event. $\square$

{[}Established{]} The likelihood-ratio supermartingale argument is the
sequential principle formalized in Kirschner et al.'s Theorem 2. The
support-union and selector-wrapper steps are the deductions made
explicit here.

{[}Established{]} The predictor $q_{t-1}$ need not be statistically
correct. It only needs to be a normalized density chosen predictably;
the common-support Gaussian case gives conditional expectation exactly
one, while the general calculation above gives at most one. Better
predictions generally make the confidence set smaller; validity follows
from predictability and normalization, not model-selection consistency.

\subsection{9.2 Gaussian known-variance
form}\label{gaussian-known-variance-form}

{[}Original synthesis --- proof complete{]} Suppose

$$
Y_t=x_t^\top\beta^\star+\varepsilon_t,
\qquad
\varepsilon_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},x_t\sim N(0,\sigma^2),
$$

with known $\sigma$. Let $\widetilde\beta_{t-1}$ be any predictable
regression estimate and take

$$
q_{t-1}(\cdot\mid x_t)
=N(x_t^\top\widetilde\beta_{t-1},\sigma^2).
$$

Then the confidence sequence is the loss sublevel set

$$
C_t=
\left\{\beta\in\Theta:
\sum_{s=1}^t(Y_s-x_s^\top\beta)^2
<
\sum_{s=1}^t(Y_s-x_s^\top\widetilde\beta_{s-1})^2
+2\sigma^2\log(1/\alpha)
\right\}.
$$

{[}Deduction{]} If $\Theta=\mathbb R^p$, the support union is
generally vacuous. If $\Theta=\Theta_{k,b,B}$, the coordinate query is
a sparse quadratic feasibility problem and can omit variables once all
beta-min alternatives involving them have cumulative loss above the
prequential threshold.

\subsection{9.3 Lasso-driven instance}\label{lasso-driven-instance}

\textbf{Corollary 5A {[}Original synthesis --- proof complete;
Lasso-specific{]}.} At time $t-1$, fit a deterministic-tie Lasso using
only past data, with $\lambda_{t-1}$ chosen by any rule measurable
with respect to past data, including past-only cross-validation. Use its
predictive Gaussian density as $q_{t-1}$, and let
$A_t=\widehat S_{\mathrm{Lasso},t}$. Then Theorem 5 gives an
anytime-valid support upper sequence.

{[}Established{]} This does not prove
$U_t=\widehat S_{\mathrm{Lasso},t}$. The certificate is the enlarged
set obtained from the likelihood confidence sequence. A Lasso-excluded
variable is certified inactive only if it is also absent from every
parameter in $C_t$.

{[}Established{]} Choosing $\lambda_t$ after observing $Y_t$ is
valid for the next prediction but cannot be retroactively used in the
factor for $Y_t$. Predictability is the exact optional-stopping
safeguard.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{9.4 Exact-$\ell_0$-driven
instance}{9.4 Exact-\textbackslash ell\_0-driven instance}}\label{exact-ell_0-driven-instance}

\textbf{Corollary 5B {[}Original synthesis --- proof complete;
exact-$\ell_0$-specific{]}.} At time $t-1$, compute a
deterministic-tie exact global $\ell_0$-constrained estimator using
only past data and use its predictive density as $q_{t-1}$. Let
$A_t$ be its exact selected support. Then Theorem 5 gives an
anytime-valid enlarged support sequence.

{[}Established{]} An approximate best-subset predictor may also be used
in the numerator because any normalized predictable density preserves
validity. It must, however, be labeled approximate, and its selected set
is not the exact $\ell_0$ solution. Approximation can make the
confidence sequence wider through worse predictive loss but does not
invalidate the supermartingale argument.

{[}Established{]} Exact best subset can improve predictive numerator
quality in some regimes and can directly solve the nonconvex
coordinate-feasibility problems. It remains worst-case combinatorial.

\subsection{9.5 Unknown variance and joint nuisance
parameters}\label{unknown-variance-and-joint-nuisance-parameters}

{[}Original synthesis --- proof complete{]} In Theorem 5, take

$$
p_{\beta,\sigma}(y\mid x)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}
\exp\left[-\frac{(y-x^\top\beta)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right]
$$

and let $\Theta$ include $\sigma$ in a prespecified positive range.
Any predictable Gaussian density with its own positive mean and scale
can be used as $q_{t-1}$. The same likelihood-ratio proof gives a
joint confidence sequence for $(\beta,\sigma)$, and the support union
is taken over its $\beta$-components.

{[}Established{]} This is an exact conceptual treatment of unknown
variance, not a claim of a closed-form sparse confidence set.
Computation now involves nonconvex optimization over support,
coefficients, and scale. Lindon et al.'s invariant construction offers a
sharper closed-form treatment for low-dimensional Gaussian linear
restrictions, while the generic joint-likelihood construction is more
flexible but potentially wider.

\subsection{9.6 Fixed-sample split-likelihood
version}\label{fixed-sample-split-likelihood-version}

\textbf{Corollary 5C {[}Deduction --- proof complete; assumption levels
1--3{]}.} Split the observations into training set $A$ and inference
set $B$. Assume that, conditional on the inference covariates and the
model parameter, the responses in $B$ have the product likelihood used
below. Fit any sparse predictor on $A$, define

$$
E_B(\theta)=
\prod_{i\in B}
\frac{p_{\widehat\theta_A}(Y_i\mid x_i)}
     {p_\theta(Y_i\mid x_i)},
\qquad
C_B=\{\theta:E_B(\theta)<1/\alpha\},
$$

and map $C_B$ to a support upper set. Conditional on the training data
and inference covariates, $E_B(\theta^\star)$ has expectation at most
one, and exactly one under common support, so the support certificate
has finite-sample coverage at least $1-\alpha$.

{[}Established{]} This is a direct universal-inference construction. It
spends data on independence and can be stabilized by averaging valid
e-values over independently randomized splits. Products or arbitrary
data-dependent choices among split e-values are not automatically valid.

\section{10. What remains to prove for
informativeness}\label{what-remains-to-prove-for-informativeness}

{[}Established{]} The coverage proofs above are complete. A useful rate
theorem would additionally need a lower bound separating every parameter
with a false active coordinate from the true mean. A representative
design-separation quantity for a restricted parameter class is

$$
\Delta_j^2
=
\inf_{\beta\in\Theta:\ |\beta_j|\ge b}
\frac1n\lVert X(\beta-\beta^\star)\rVert_2^2.
$$

{[}Deduction{]} If $\Delta_j=0$, no residual- or likelihood-based
method can reliably exclude coordinate $j$ because an admissible
active-$j$ parameter produces the same or arbitrarily close mean.
Positive $\Delta_j$ is a support-specific identifiability requirement.

{[}Conjecture{]} Under predictable designs satisfying a time-growing
version of $\Delta_{j,t}^2$, conditionally sub-Gaussian noise, and an
online predictor with sparse regret $R_t=O(k\log t)$, the prequential
certificate should eventually exclude every inactive $j$ for which the
cumulative separation dominates $R_t+\log(1/\alpha)$. A complete proof
requires a uniform lower-tail bound over the nonconvex active-$j$
parameter class and is not provided here.

{[}Established{]} A practical study should report coverage, $|U_n|$,
$|Z_n|$, inactive-feature certification rate, time to first
certification, and solver optimality gaps. Coverage alone is
insufficient because $U=[p]$ is always valid.

\section{13. Counterexamples and failure
modes}\label{counterexamples-and-failure-modes}

{[}Original result --- proof complete{]} \textbf{Continuous
alternatives.} Theorem 1 shows that exact-zero certification has
probability at most $\alpha$ at a zero that can be approached by
active alternatives. This rules out uniformly powerful exact-zero
certificates over unrestricted continuous coefficient spaces.

{[}Original result --- proof complete{]} \textbf{Confidence-ellipsoid
vacuity.} Any full-dimensional coefficient confidence set has support
union $[p]$, irrespective of how small its volume is.

{[}Deduction{]} \textbf{Full-model collection vacuity.} Any
model-confidence collection containing $[p]$ has union $[p]$. Exact
model coverage does not imply informative inactive-feature
certification.

{[}Original result --- proof complete{]} \textbf{Collinear
representation.} If $X_1=X_2$, the parameters
$(\beta_1,\beta_2)=(a,0)$ and $(0,a)$ induce the same mean. Uniform
support inference over both cannot reliably certify either duplicated
feature as inactive.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Misspecified sparsity.} If an exact-$\ell_0$
method restricts all candidate supports to size $k<s^\star$, no
support upper set formed solely from those candidates can cover
$S^\star$.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Cross-validation transfer error.} A selective
or recovery theorem for fixed $\lambda$ does not automatically cover
cross-validated $\lambda$. A prequential construction remains valid
only when the value used to predict $Y_t$ is chosen before $Y_t$ is
observed.

{[}Established{]} \textbf{Approximate-versus-exact optimization.} An
approximate support can be used as a predictor or as an arbitrary
wrapper set, but it cannot be reported as the exact global $\ell_0$
solution. Solver gaps must be included in any claim about exact
best-subset output.

\section{14. Recommended research
program}\label{recommended-research-program}

{[}Deduction{]} The first implementable benchmark should use assumption
level 1 with a prespecified $\Theta_{k,b,B}$. Compute the
residual-ball certificate, the exact-size RSS model collection when
$k=s^\star$ is known, and the prequential likelihood certificate.
Compare all three to $U=[p]$, the raw Lasso support, and the raw
exact-$\ell_0$ support.

{[}Deduction{]} The second benchmark should vary $b$, support size,
column coherence, and the restricted separation $\Delta_j$. The
purpose is to identify the phase transition from exact but vacuous
coverage to useful exclusion.

{[}Deduction{]} The third step should replace known $\sigma$ by an
independent or jointly modeled scale and compare the generic likelihood
construction with Wang et al.'s conditional nuisance elimination.

{[}Deduction{]} The fourth step should implement online Lasso and online
exact-$\ell_0$ predictors in Theorem 5. The same support parameter
space should be used so that differences in $|U_t|$ reflect predictive
regret and computation rather than different validity assumptions.

{[}Conjecture{]} The most promising theoretical rate result is an
eventual-exclusion theorem under beta-min, cumulative restricted
eigenvalue/separation, and bounded online regret. The key inequality
should compare the best active-$j$ likelihood to the prequential
predictor's likelihood uniformly over all inactive $j$.

\section{17. Final assessment}\label{final-assessment}

{[}Established{]} A correct answer to the original inferential question
must not be phrased as ``run Lasso and trust the zeros.'' The raw sparse
selector and the confidence certificate are separate objects.

{[}Established{]} Exact fixed-sample support coverage is available
through three routes identified here: an exact model-confidence
collection followed by a support union; an exact restricted parameter
confidence set followed by a support union; or an exact likelihood-ratio
confidence set followed by a support union. Wang, Xie, and Zhang provide
the strongest established high-dimensional model-confidence result. The
residual and prequential constructions give transparent alternative
routes.

{[}Original result --- proof complete{]} The Lasso and exact $\ell_0$
estimators can be incorporated without selection-validity assumptions by
using them as arbitrary wrapper sets or predictable likelihood
predictors. Their optimization quality affects informativeness, not the
exact coverage proof.

{[}Established{]} Without support identifiability or a separation
condition, the exact-zero target is nonregular and useful uniform
certification is impossible at the boundary. Any scientifically
defensible method must state the separation model explicitly and report
how certificate size changes as the separation weakens.

{[}Search limitation{]} No audited source was found that already
provides all of the following in one practical method: high-dimensional
$p\ge n$; fixed finite $n$; exact user-calibrated coverage at fixed
finite computational budget; unknown variance; data-adaptive tuning; a
nonvacuous upper support set; and anytime validity under adaptive
covariates. The constructions in this report satisfy exact coverage
under explicit subsets of these assumptions, while computation and
informativeness remain the central research tasks.
