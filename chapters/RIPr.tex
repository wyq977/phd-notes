Everything should be very concrete in 3D case, we do not assume any independence.z
The sample space is
\begin{equation}
  \Omega = \{0,1\}^3 = \left\{000,001,010,100,011,101,110,111\right\}
\end{equation}

Any probability measure $P$ on $\Omega$ can be identified with a point in the simplex
\begin{equation}
\left\{
p\in\mathbb R^8:
p_x\ge 0,\ \sum_{x\in\Omega} p_x=1
\right\}.
\end{equation}

Since we do not assume independence, parameter space is 
\begin{equation}
  \Theta\subset\Rf^8.
\end{equation}

We write $P_\theta$ for some element $\theta$ in $\Theta$.

Questions:

Then if I have prior $W$ on parameter space $\Theta$,
it should follow that the mixture distribution is still 
\begin{equation}
  P_{W} = \int_{\Theta} P_{\theta} \dd{W}.
\end{equation}

Putting a prior in this case

A prior $W$ is a probability distribution on the parameter space $\Theta$.
It induces a mixture distribution on the sample space by
\[
P_W(x)
=
\int_\Theta P_\theta(x)\,\mathrm d W(\theta).
\]
In the full multi-Bernoulli model, $P_\theta(x)=\theta_x$, and hence
\[
P_W(x)
=
\int_\Theta \theta_x\,\mathrm d W(\theta)
=
\mathbb E_W[\theta_x].
\]
Thus $P_W=P_{\bar\theta}$, where
\[
\bar\theta=\int_\Theta \theta\,\mathrm d W(\theta)\in\Theta.
\]
Equivalently, $P_W=P_{\delta_{\bar\theta}}$, although generally
$W\neq \delta_{\bar\theta}$. Thus different priors may induce the same
mixture distribution.

We consider a description gain given by
\begin{equation*}

\subsection{The Frank Wolfe Algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}
	\DontPrintSemicolon
	\caption{The Frank Wolfe Algorithm}
	\BlankLine
	Let $Q_1 = {\argmin}_{Q \in \Qc}\, \KL{Q}{P}$\;
	\For{$k = 2, 3, \dots, K$}{
		Let $\alpha_k=\frac{2}{k+1}$\;
		Find $\theta_k$ that minizes $\KL{Q}{(1-\alpha_k) Q_{k-1} + \alpha_k Q_{\theta}}$\;
		Let $Q_k=(1-\alpha_k) Q_{k-1} + \alpha_k Q_{}$
	}
\end{algorithm}

Here, the distribution $Q'$ and $\alpha$ is chosen (coupled) such that the divergence is minimized.
The minimizer need not be unique.

\textbf{Regularity condition on alternative}

Li's algorithm is apparently greedy with high fluctuation in initial steps.
Additionally, this task is computationally expensive, and it is not clear of the convexity.

\textbf{Is the returned mixture in the convex hull?}

The returned $Q_{(m)}$ is in the convex hull.
The first step returned a single element in $\Qc$ with the smallest KL divergence.
Iteratively, the linear combination is still in the convex hull, i.e.
\begin{align*}
	Q_{(2)} & = \alpha_1 \cdot Q_{(1)} + (1 - \alpha_1)\cdot Q_{(1)}' \\
	Q_{(2)} & = \alpha_2 \cdot Q_{(2)} + (1 - \alpha_2)\cdot Q_{(2)}' \\
	        & = \alpha_2 \cdot \alpha_1 \cdot Q_{(1)} +
	\alpha_2 \cdot (1 - \alpha_1) \cdot Q_{(1)}' +
	(1 - \alpha_2)\cdot Q_{(2)}'.
\end{align*}

It is clear that $Q_{(2)}$ or $Q_{(m)}$ would still be a convex combination of elements in $\Qc$.
